What’s new in Gink

Updates to the apps, the web and the Developer API, newest first.

27 November 2026

Share a link, and Gink sees it

Paste a link to a Facebook reel, an Instagram post, a TikTok, a YouTube video, an X or Bluesky post, a song, a news story, a PDF or a picture, and Gink opens it before it answers. It knows what it is and who posted it, reads its caption and its words, and looks at its picture to say what it shows. Ask “who posted it?” a moment later and it still knows.

Gink is also honest about what it can’t see. Most social sites don’t let other apps play their videos, so for a reel Gink sees what a messaging app’s preview shows — the caption, the cover, who posted it — and tells you so. Save the video and attach it, and Gink watches the whole thing.

The web is treated as data, never as instructions. Words a page hides from people, often written for AI assistants, are taken out, and a page that tries to tell Gink what to do is flagged and ignored. Only public addresses are opened, and every redirect and picture is checked first. For developers: links in the API, both SDKs and gink read.

21 November 2026

Ask for something, and Gink makes it

“Make me anything” used to get a question back, then three more. Now Gink chooses something good, makes it and says in a line what it chose, and “add more to it” or “continue” carry straight on. Question cards are kept for what only you know, and typing your own answer on one sends it, with the arrow or with Enter.

Artifacts can be more things now: a React app you can click through, a chart drawn from its numbers, a table you can sort by any column (and open in Excel or Sheets), and slides you can present full screen. Pages and apps are made to work on a phone and in dark mode, with real content.

Long chats keep working. A chat that made a page three times used to grow too big for the AI and stop with an error full of account details; now only the newest version goes along, a request too big for one model goes to one that takes more, and when the AI is busy you’re told in plain words when to try again. Opening a chat on the web shows it formatted at once, with your answers to its questions in place.

21 November 2026

Ask once. Gink keeps working.

You can now give Gink a task for later: “every morning at 8, give me the three biggest stories”, “each Monday, plan my week”, “on Friday at 17:00, remind me to send the report”. Gink’s server does it at that time, in your time zone, even when your apps are closed, searching the web as it needs. The result arrives in the task’s own chat on all your devices, with a notification, and by email if you like. Ask in any chat, or add one in Settings › Scheduled tasks, where you can pause it, run it now or change it.

When Gink makes something complete, a web page or small app, a logo, a diagram, a letter or a code file, it now appears as an artifact: a card in the chat that opens beside it. Try the page live, read its code, go back to an earlier version, copy it, download it or open it in a tab of its own.

Gink also reads more by itself. Paste a link and it reads the page rather than guessing; when an answer needs today’s facts, it searches without being asked. Mention a GitHub repository and the answer comes from its real code, and on your computer Gink can map a project, find where things are, make a change with a backup first and run the project’s own tests. Harder questions get more thought, and bigger pieces of work more room.

Signing in has a new look on the web and in the apps, and Gink Pro now includes 25 scheduled tasks, ones that run every hour too. For developers: scheduled tasks and artifacts, the web and code in the API, both SDKs and the gink command.

20 November 2026

Real maps, and a Gink that knows your device

Maps in answers are now real street maps, drawn on your device from OpenStreetMap: rivers, parks, buildings, stations and street names, light or dark to match Gink. Each place is marked and named, and the cards under the map show its photo, what kind of place it is and a Directions button that opens your own maps app. Zoom in to the buildings, or open the map bigger.

Gink now knows which device it’s running on, so “which computer am I using?” gets a straight answer. Say “do a check” and it checks your computer itself: system and updates, storage, memory, battery health, what’s using the processor and whether protection is on, then tells you what to do about anything it found.

Code in answers looks cleaner, with its language at the top and copy and download one click away, in the chat and in Gink Genie. On Macs with macOS Catalina, Gink now asks for the microphone, so Hey Gink hears you once you update.

20 November 2026

Voice messages, maps and your screen

Hold the microphone to send a voice message instead of typing. Gink writes down what you said, in any language, and answers out loud.

When an answer is about places, a map now appears in the chat by itself, with numbered pins and a list. Drag it, zoom in, or open a place. There’s no button to press.

In Live, you can share your screen instead of your camera: say “look at my screen” and ask about what’s in front of you. Gink sees it only while you share it, and only when you ask. Screen view and the camera are part of Gink Pro.

20 November 2026

An agent that finishes the job

Ask for something with several steps, like “open my portfolio in VS Code and fix the broken link”, and Gink now writes a plan you can watch, then works through it on its own. It finds, opens and closes apps, opens projects in your code editor, and reads, writes and edits text files, keeping a backup of every file it changes. If it stops with steps still open, it keeps going.

Managed agents in the Developer API work the same way, with up to 60 steps.

20 November 2026

A memory you control

With Generate memory from chats, Gink remembers lasting things you mention, like a move or a new job, and shows you each one with Undo. Sensitive topics, such as health or religion, stay out unless you turn on Include sensitive topics in memory.

Used another AI assistant before? Choose Import memory from other AI providers in Settings > Memory: copy one prompt into it, then paste its answer into Gink.

Settings also has Location metadata, to choose whether answers use your device’s time zone and language, and Privacy choices, with every policy one tap away. Read how memory works.

15 October 2026

Gink can now work your computer

Ask Gink to open an app or a folder in Finder, open Terminal in a project, skip a song, change the volume, take a screenshot, find a file or tell you how much battery you have. Common requests work instantly, even without the AI.

14 October 2026

Say “Hey Gink”

You can now wake Gink without touching anything. Say “Hey Gink”, and the edges of the window light up while Gink listens. Ask your question, hear the answer, and carry on with a follow-up without saying the wake word again.

Listening for the wake word happens entirely on your computer, so no audio leaves it until you say “Hey Gink”. It's off until you switch it on in Settings, under Voice.

13 October 2026

A morning briefing that remembers what you were doing

Alongside the weather, your plans and a headline, the briefing now picks up where you left off: tasks you're partway through, with the next step, yesterday's conversations, and the files you were editing. The task closest to done gets a nudge to finish it first.

12 October 2026

Gink now sounds like a person

Gink's voices are now natural and expressive, with eleven personalities, including Nigerian, Kenyan, British and French voices. In voice conversations, Gink speaks like a person too: short sentences, no lists read aloud, and it starts answering after the first sentence instead of making you wait for the whole reply.
