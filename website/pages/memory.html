How Gink’s memory works

Settings > Memory, in the apps and on the web

Gink can remember lasting things about you — where you live, your work, how you like answers — so you don’t have to repeat them. You decide: everything below can be turned off, and any memory can be forgotten.

Telling Gink to remember

Say “remember that my exams start on 12 November” in any chat. A single thing said again replaces the old one: tell Gink you moved to another city and the new city takes the old one’s place. Memories follow your account to all your devices.

Generate memory from chats

With Generate memory from chats on (it is, until you turn it off), Gink also remembers lasting things you mention in passing, by itself:

  • Right away, when a message plainly states a lasting fact (“I live in Accra”, “I’m vegetarian”).
  • Every few messages, Gink’s AI reads the recent conversation and picks out at most five lasting facts about you — never one-off requests, guesses, what Gink said, or details about other people.

Each time, Gink shows what it remembered, with Undo. It never does this in a temporary chat, and never in a workspace in healthcare mode. Passwords, codes, card and account numbers, and anything about self-harm, abuse or crime are never kept by themselves, whatever your settings.

Include sensitive topics in memory

Sensitive topics — health conditions, allergies and medicines, religious beliefs, sexuality, political views and the like — are left out of memories Gink makes by itself, unless you turn on Include sensitive topics in memory. With it on, Gink may keep what you plainly said about yourself (“I’m allergic to penicillin”), so answers can take it into account. It stays off for accounts of people under 18 and in healthcare workspaces. Food choices like vegetarian, halal or kosher aren’t treated as sensitive.

Import memory from other AI providers

Used another AI assistant before? Bring what it knows about you:

  1. In Gink, open Settings > Memory and choose Start import next to “Import memory from other AI providers”.
  2. Copy the prompt Gink shows, and send it in a chat with your other assistant. It asks for one lasting fact per line, and leaves out secrets.
  3. Copy its whole answer, paste it into Gink and choose Add to memory.

Gink adds each lasting fact, turns “The user lives in…” into “I live in…”, skips what it already knows and anything it never keeps, and skips sensitive topics unless you turned them on. Check the list in Settings > Memory afterwards and forget anything you don’t want.

Developers: GET /v1/memories/import-prompt, then POST /v1/memories/import with the answer; in Python client.memories.import_text(answer), in JavaScript gink.memories.import(answer), or gink memories import answer.txt in a terminal. POST /v1/memories/generate picks the lasting facts out of a conversation.

Seeing and forgetting

  • Settings > Memory lists everything Gink remembers: forget one, or forget everything.
  • Say “forget that I live in Lima” in a chat.
  • Turn off Use memories in answers (Settings > Privacy) to keep your memories but stop Gink from using them.

Where memories are kept

On your devices and, when you’re signed in, with your account on Gink’s server so they reach your other devices. When Gink uses its AI to pick memories, the recent messages go through Gink’s server to the AI service that answers you, just like a normal answer; nothing else is kept. More in the privacy policy.
