Responsible disclosure policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Found a security problem in Gink? Thank you. Please tell us privately first, give us a fair chance to fix it, and don’t harm anyone’s data while you look. If you do that, we won’t take legal action against you.

What this covers

  • The Gink apps for Mac, Windows, Linux, iPhone, iPad and Android, and Gink Genie.
  • The Gink server, including Gink on the web (/app), the developer console, the Developer API (/v1), managed agents, scheduled tasks and webhooks.
  • The Gink SDKs and command-line tool (gink-ai for Python and JavaScript), and this website.

Services we use (for example our AI, email, payment and hosting providers) have their own disclosure programmes: report problems in their systems to them. If you’re not sure, report it to us and we’ll pass it on.

How to report

  • Best: GitHub’s private reporting on our repository — report a vulnerability.
  • Or email gink@ginkaisupport.com with “Security” in the subject.

Please include what you found, where (the app or page, its version, your platform), the steps to reproduce it, and what an attacker could do with it. Screenshots, a short video or a proof of concept help a lot. Never put the details in a public issue, a review or social media before we’ve fixed it.

What we promise

  • We acknowledge your report within 48 hours, and tell you within 7 days what we think of it.
  • We keep you updated while we fix it, and tell you when the fix is released.
  • We aim to fix serious problems within 30 days and others within 90 days. If it takes longer, we explain why.
  • With your permission, we thank you by name in the release notes. We don’t run a paid bug bounty at the moment.
  • We won’t take legal action against you, or ask anyone else to, for research that follows this policy. If someone else does, we’ll make it clear that you acted with our permission.

Rules for testing

  • Use only accounts you own, or accounts whose owners agreed. Never try to read, change or delete other people’s chats, files, memories or accounts. If you reach someone else’s data by accident, stop, don’t keep a copy, and tell us.
  • Do as little as needed to show the problem: no mass downloads, no keeping access, no moving deeper into our systems.
  • Don’t slow down or break the service for others: no denial-of-service, load or spam testing, and respect the rate limits.
  • No social engineering, phishing or physical attacks on people who work on Gink, and no attacks on our providers.
  • Don’t share what you found with anyone until we’ve released a fix, or 90 days have passed since your report, whichever comes first (tell us before you publish).

Usually not in scope

  • Reports from automated scanners with no real impact shown.
  • Missing security headers or cookie flags where nothing can actually be exploited.
  • Clickjacking on pages with no sensitive action, and “self-XSS” that needs the victim to paste code themselves.
  • Problems that need a phone or computer that is already unlocked and in the attacker’s hands, or a jailbroken or rooted device.
  • An AI answer you got Gink to write (a “jailbreak”) — please tell us at gink@ginkaisupport.com instead, unless it leaks other people’s data, keys or Gink’s system access, which is in scope.

Keys found by mistake

If you come across a key, password or token (ours or anyone’s), don’t use it: tell us straight away so it can be revoked. See also our security policy on GitHub.
